Our Background

GREEN ROOTS was developed as a student project to promote awareness of the importance of forests and responsible environmental practices. As students who recognize the value of natural resources, we want to share knowledge about Philippine tree species, forest conservation, and community participation. Through this initiative, we hope to inspire simple but meaningful actions that contribute to a sustainable environment.

Our Vision

A future where people and nature grow together, where forests are protected, biodiversity safety, and communities actively participate in environmental conservation.

Our Mission

To promote forest conservation, encourage responsible tree planting, educate communities, and inspire individuals to become active environmental stewards.

WHY FORESTS MATTER

More than trees,
they are life itself.

Forests are essential to the balance of our environment. They absorb carbon dioxide, help regulate the climate, protect watersheds, reduce soil erosion, and provide homes for countless species of plants and animals. They also support the livelihoods and cultural traditions of many communities. When forests are destroyed, the effects reach far beyond the trees themselves. Protecting forests means protecting biodiversity, natural resources, and the future of the next generation.

KNOW OUR NATIVE TREES

Meet the trees that
make our forests thrive.

Discover four important tree species associated with Philippine forestry.

01
PHILIPPINE NATIONAL TREE

Narra

Pterocarpus indicus

Narra is a large indigenous Philippine tree known for its beautiful wood and spreading crown. It grows best in areas receiving abundant sunlight.

Ecological Importance

Provides shade, supports biodiversity, and contributes to forest restoration. It is classified as Critically Endangered in the Philippines' NPDC species profile.

02
INDIGENOUS TREE

Molave

Vitex parviflora

Molave is a native hardwood tree recognized for its durable timber and spreading crown. It naturally occurs in lowland forests, including areas with a distinct dry season.

Ecological Importance

Offers shade and habitat for wildlife. Its flowers also provide resources for pollinating insects.

03
PHILIPPINE ENDEMIC

White Lauan

Shorea contorta

White Lauan is a tall Philippine-endemic dipterocarp tree. It is commonly associated with lowland forest ecosystems.

Ecological Importance

Forms part of tropical forest communities and provides habitat and food resources for forest organisms.

04
HIGHLAND TREE

Benguet Pine

Pinus kesiya

Also called Saleng, Benguet Pine is a characteristic tree of the highlands of Northern Luzon. It grows naturally in open mountain areas.

Ecological Importance

Helps protect mountain slopes and contributes to watershed protection and the identity of Cordillera landscapes.

FROM IDEAS TO ACTION

Growing change through
meaningful action.

Conservation becomes stronger when knowledge turns into action.

PROGRAM 01

Tree Planting Campaign

Organize tree-planting activities using appropriate native species. The program promotes vegetation recovery, provides potential wildlife habitat, and encourages community involvement.

PROGRAM 02

Forest Restoration

Support the recovery of degraded forest areas through site assessment, assisted natural regeneration, native tree planting, and regular monitoring of growing seedlings.

PROGRAM 03

Environmental Awareness

Conduct educational seminars and information campaigns to help students and communities understand biodiversity, forest protection, and responsible environmental practices.
